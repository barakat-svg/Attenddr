Two questions every school has to answer

Who showed up. And whether anyone actually learned. Both are surprisingly hard to see once classes get large and days get busy.

On attendance

  • Roll calls take teaching time, especially in large classes.
  • Records are manual and difficult to audit.
  • Students can slip below the roughly 70% exam-eligibility threshold before anyone notices.
  • Parents may be the last to know when their child misses school.

On learning

  • Lessons are delivered once and are hard to revisit afterward.
  • Teachers often build quizzes and revision material by hand.
  • There's no quick way to see what a class retained that day.
  • Parents may only see a grade at the end of term.

One terminal, two jobs

The same device at the door handles both — a live record of who's in the room, and a live record of what was taught in it.

What's actually on the wall

No app to install on every phone. One station at the door, and one dashboard in the office.

  • Android tablet station — mounted at the classroom door.
  • NFC card reader — for fast tap-in.
  • Face-recognition camera — as a second way to check in at the gate.
  • Wireless microphone — picks up the lesson from anywhere in the room.
  • Web dashboard — a real-time, multi-campus view for the school office.

Built for school offices, not individual users

Attendr is bought by schools and institutions — from a single school to a large fee-paying group.

  • Best fit: multi-campus networks, where roll call and revision prep are a real burden across many classrooms.
  • Initial market: Nigeria first, then multi-campus international networks in the Gulf.
  • Used day to day by: teachers, students, and tech-conscious parents.

Two people carry the accountability at every school: the office that has to prove who was there, and the classroom that has to prove something was learned. Attendr sits at the point where both happen — the door.

Why schools buy it

  • Protects exam eligibility, with fewer disputes and less liability.
  • Frees up teaching time and keeps records clean for inspections.
  • Cuts teacher prep work through generated quizzes and revision material.
  • Shows parents and inspectors what was actually taught, not just a grade.
  • Opens a path to paid parent services, like the weekly digest and quiz access.
